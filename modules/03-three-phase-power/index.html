Start with the idea: what makes a system three-phase?

A three-phase AC system has three sinusoidal voltages of the same frequency, usually with equal magnitudes in a balanced system. Their phase angles are separated by 120 electrical degrees. This arrangement is widely used because it can transfer power smoothly and efficiently.

In an ideal balanced three-phase system, total instantaneous power delivered to a balanced load is constant. This is one reason three-phase systems are useful for motors and power delivery.

Real, reactive, and apparent power

Real power P does useful work and is measured in kW. Reactive power Q moves energy back and forth in electric and magnetic fields and is measured in kvar. Under the convention used here, lagging inductive loads have positive Q and leading capacitive loads have negative Q. Apparent power S combines the two and is measured in kVA.

S = PPF; |Q| = P tan(cos⁻¹(PF))
Quick check: If P = 100 kW and PF = 1.0, what is S?
Reveal answer

S = 100 kVA. For an ideal unity-power-factor load, Q = 0 kvar.

Core formula

S = P / PF; |Q| = P tan(cos⁻¹(PF))
Qc = P[tan(cos⁻¹(PFi)) − tan(cos⁻¹(PFt))]
Δ: Cphase = Qc3ωVLL²; Y: Cphase = QcωVLL²

Worked example — power quantities

Given: P = 100 kW and PF = 0.80 lagging.

Find: apparent power S and reactive-power magnitude |Q|.

Formula: S = P/PF; |Q| = P tan(cos⁻¹(PF)).

Substitution: S = 100/0.80 = 125 kVA; φ = cos⁻¹(0.80) = 36.87°.

Calculation: |Q| = 100 × tan(36.87°) = 75 kvar.

Final answer: S = 125 kVA; Q = +75 kvar under the lagging-load convention.

Interpretation: the load uses 100 kW of real power and draws 75 kvar of inductive reactive power.

Assumptions and limitations

Sign convention: lagging inductive loads are positive Q; leading capacitive loads are negative Q. Capacitor sizing must state connection (wye/delta), line-to-line voltage, frequency, and target power factor. The capacitance shown is per phase; total bank kvar is the full three-phase requirement.

Independent practice

  1. A balanced three-phase load consumes 60 kW at 0.80 lagging power factor. Find apparent power and reactive-power magnitude.
  2. A 100 kW load improves from 0.80 to 0.95 power factor. Estimate the required capacitor-bank rating.
  3. In one sentence, explain why the capacitance per phase differs between wye and delta banks for the same kvar and line voltage.
Reveal answer guide
  1. S = 60/0.80 = 75 kVA; Q = 60 tan(cos⁻¹0.80) = 45 kvar lagging.
  2. Qc = 100[tan(cos⁻¹0.80) − tan(cos⁻¹0.95)] ≈ 42.13 kvar.
  3. The phase voltage differs by connection: wye phase voltage is VLL/√3, while delta phase voltage equals VLL.
