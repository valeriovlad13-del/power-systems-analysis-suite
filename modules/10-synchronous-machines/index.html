Core formula

Ns = 120fP; Pmax = 3EVX sin δ (phase RMS E and V in volts)

Worked example

A 4-pole machine on 60 Hz has synchronous speed Ns = 120 × 604 = 1800 rpm.

Assumptions and limitations

The power-angle relationship is an idealized classical model. It does not represent a complete transient-stability simulation or machine capability assessment.

The E, V, and X inputs are per-phase quantities; use consistent SI units.

Theory & formulas

Speed, EMF, power angle, and stability

Synchronous speed Ns=120f/P rpm for frequency f in Hz and P poles.

Practice problem

A four-pole synchronous machine operates at 60 Hz. E = 8,000 V/phase, V = 7,200 V/phase, and X = 8 Ω/phase. Find synchronous speed and classical maximum power.

Check your result

Ns = 120 × 604 = 1,800 rpm. Pmax = 3 × 8,000 × 7,2008 = 21,600,000 W = 21.6 MW, using the idealized power-angle model.

Scope and assumptions

The power-angle estimate assumes a classical model with negligible armature resistance and consistent per-phase RMS quantities. It is not a transient-stability study.
