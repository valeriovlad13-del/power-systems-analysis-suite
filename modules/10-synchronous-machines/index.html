Core formula

Ns = 120fP; Pmax = 3EVX sin δ (phase RMS E and V in volts)

Worked example: Synchronous speed and maximum power

Problem statement: A four-pole synchronous machine operates at 60 Hz. Its internal EMF is 8,000 V/phase, terminal voltage is 7,200 V/phase, and transfer reactance is 8 Ω/phase. Find synchronous speed and classical maximum power.

Given: f = 60 Hz, P = 4 poles, E = 8,000 V/phase, V = 7,200 V/phase, X = 8 Ω/phase.

Find: Ns and Pmax.

Formula: Ns = 120f/P; Pmax = 3EVX for the idealized model at δ = 90°.

Substitution: f = 60 Hz, P = 4 poles, E = 8,000 V/phase, V = 7,200 V/phase, X = 8 Ω/phase.

Step-by-step calculation:

Formula: Ns = 120f/P; Pmax = 3EV/X for the idealized model at δ = 90°.

Substitution: f = 60 Hz, P = 4 poles, E = 8,000 V/phase, V = 7,200 V/phase, X = 8 Ω/phase.

Step-by-step calculation:

  1. Synchronous speed: Ns = 120fP = 120 × 604 = 1,800 rpm.
  2. Classical power-angle relation: P = 3EVX sin δ. The ideal maximum occurs at δ = 90°, where sin δ = 1.
  3. Maximum power: Pmax = 3 × 8,000 × 7,2008 = 21,600,000 W = 21.6 MW.

Final answer: Ns = 1,800 rpm; idealized Pmax = 21.6 MW.

Interpretation: This is the classical steady-state power-angle limit under the stated assumptions, not a complete transient-stability or machine capability study.

Scope, assumptions & limitations

The power-angle estimate assumes a classical model with negligible armature resistance and consistent per-phase RMS quantities. It is not a transient-stability study.
