Core formula

Isec = IprimaryCT ratio; t = 0.14 × TMS(I/Ip)^0.02 − 1 (IEC standard inverse)

Worked example: CT ratio and inverse-time relay

Problem statement: A 600:5 current transformer feeds a relay with 1 A secondary pickup and TMS = 0.1. If primary current is 1,200 A, calculate the ideal secondary current, current multiple, and IEC standard-inverse operating-time estimate.

Given: CT = 600:5, Iprimary = 1,200 A, pickup = 1 A secondary, TMS = 0.1.

Find: CT ratio, Isecondary, current multiple M, and t.

  1. CT ratio = 6005 = 120:1.
  2. Ideal secondary current = 1,200120 = 10 A.
  3. Current multiple M = IsecondaryIpickup = 101 = 10.
  4. IEC standard-inverse estimate: t = 0.14 × TMSM0.02 − 1 = 0.14 × 0.1100.02 − 1 ≈ 0.299 s.

Final answer: Isecondary = 10 A, M = 10, estimated operating time ≈ 0.299 s.

Interpretation: This is an idealized curve estimate. Confirm the selected relay manufacturer's curve, CT performance, breaker clearing time, and coordination margins before setting real protection.

Assumptions and limitations

The inverse-time result uses the IEC standard-inverse equation as an educational estimate. Real coordination requires validated relay curves, breaker clearing time, CT performance, equipment limits, and applicable standards.

The time estimate is only meaningful above pickup and must be checked against the selected relay manufacturer’s actual curve.
